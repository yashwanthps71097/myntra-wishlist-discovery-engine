## Problem Statement

### **Myntra cannot clearly identify why high-intent wishlist items fail to convert into purchases**

Myntra users frequently save fashion products to their wishlist because they like the product, may need it for a future occasion, or want to consider it later. However, many wishlisted products remain unpurchased for 30 days or longer.

The underlying reasons for this drop-off are not directly visible from wishlist data alone.

Users may delay purchases because of **price uncertainty, waiting for an occasion, lack of necessity, comparison with alternatives, fit or size concerns, insufficient reviews, or simply using the wishlist as a bookmarking tool**.

### **Discovery Problem**

> **How might we use AI to analyze large-scale user conversations and uncover, quantify, and prioritize the underlying reasons why Myntra users postpone purchasing products they have wishlisted?**

The AI Discovery Engine will analyze publicly available conversations across these sources:
* **App Store reviews**
* **Play Store reviews**
* **Reddit discussions**
* **Fashion and shopping communities**
* **Social media conversations**
* **YouTube comments**
* **Product reviews and Q&A where relevant**
* **Other publicly available conversations about online fashion shopping**

to identify recurring:

* **Wishlist motivations** — Why do users save products?
* **Purchase barriers** — Why do they postpone buying?
* **Uncertainties** — What information or confidence is missing?
* **Decision patterns** — How do users compare and shortlist products?
* **User segments** — Which types of wishlist users behave differently?
* **Opportunity areas** — Which unresolved problems have the strongest potential to improve wishlist-to-purchase conversion?

### **Output of the Engine**

The goal is **not just sentiment analysis or review summarization**.

The engine should convert unstructured user conversations into:

**User Conversations → Behavioral Themes → Quantified Evidence → User Segments → Purchase Barriers → Opportunity Areas → Testable Hypotheses**

This discovery will then be validated through **5–6 primary user interviews** before defining the final product problem.
